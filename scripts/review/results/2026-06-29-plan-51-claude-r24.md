## Findings

1. **Non-convergence after 23 rounds is itself evidence the work unit is mis-sized (Adversarial Review Summary, lines 638-702).** Both active providers returned MAJOR on *every* round r3→r23 — 21 consecutive rounds — and r23 is still dual-MAJOR (line 702). Reviewers explicitly asked to "split OR explicitly accept" the broad front-loaded scope at r20, r22, and r23 (lines 689, 695, 698). The plan's response was to *accept* rather than split, but a single "T3" issue that creates five new Python modules (`ace_public_tokens.py`, `ace_public_surface_scan.py`, `ace_sampling_firewall.py`, `validate_ace_wave0_control_plane.py`, the legal wrapper), a YAML deny-list, two fixture trees, ~40 TDD tests, and modifies five skills plus three docs plus the workflow (Files to Change, lines 491-517) is several issues' worth of surface. The inability to clear two reviewers across 23 iterations is the strongest available signal that the decomposition, not the wording, is the problem.

2. **The plan structurally cannot reach `status:plan-review` without an out-of-band user waiver, and that gate is unresolved (lines 462-473, 711).** Gemini has failed noninteractive auth with `rc=41` for 21 straight rounds (r3-r23). The plan's own degraded-quorum rule says Gemini auth failure "does not automatically authorize downgrade" and `status:plan-review` is "blocked until Gemini is restored or the user explicitly approves a one-round degraded review quorum." No such restoration or approval is recorded. This is a real, currently-open blocker to the plan's advancement that the plan cannot self-resolve.

3. **#51's plan-review gate is coupled to mutating sibling issue #63 (lines 374-382, 624; Files to Change line 505).** The Pre-Label Checklist requires editing the *live #63 issue body* and patching `docs/plans/2026-06-29-issue-63-...md` before #51 can transition. #63 is an independently-open issue with its own plan and lane; gating #51's approval on edits to #63 creates a coordination hazard (concurrent edits, ownership ambiguity) that the "one-time plan patch" framing (line 378) does not fully neutralize.

4. **Pre-label scanning runs weaker patterns than #51 will eventually enforce, and the plan does not establish sufficiency (line 625).** Because `scripts/validate_ace_wave0_control_plane.py` does not exist until implementation, all pre-label public-surface scanning is delegated to the parent `validate_ace_epic_wave_coordination.py`. I confirmed that validator has leak/traversal/digest patterns — but #51 defines materially richer checks it considers necessary (executable-context classification, the good/bad fixture marker grammar, the metadata-evidence shape, `--scan-fixture-root`). The plan asserts the parent is an adequate "fallback" but provides no evidence that the parent's pattern set covers the #51-specific surfaces (e.g. `.legal-deny-list.yaml`, the new Python modules). The gate that actually runs at label time is thus looser than the one the plan specifies.

5. **Several acceptance criteria remain compound despite r23 flagging exactly this (line 698: "decomposing compound acceptance criteria").** Criteria at lines 581, 585, 589, and 594 each bundle 4-6 independent assertions into one checkbox (e.g. line 589 mixes the four-row `wave_class` matrix, the #52-#60 requirement, the ingestion-binding rejection rule, AND the #50 portfolio carve-out). A reviewer cannot check these off atomically, which undercuts their use as a pass/fail gate.

6. **`secrets.token_hex(16)` ↔ `pst_[0-9a-f]{32}` prefix is left implicit (lines 182, 188).** `secrets.token_hex(16)` yields 32 hex chars with no `pst_` prefix; the grammar requires `pst_` + 32 hex. The plan never states that `generate_public_source_token()` prepends the literal `pst_`, so an implementer could emit a 32-char token that fails the very grammar the plan mandates. Minor, but it is the one place the token contract is underspecified.
